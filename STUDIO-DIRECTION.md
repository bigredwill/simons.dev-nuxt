# Studio direction — simons.dev

Conversation notes captured September 30, 2026. This is an editorial brief, not a finished brand statement or an implementation plan.

## What prompted the conversation

Will feels that the site repeats itself and has a backlog of project writing. Home and About repeat current work, location, and personal background. The goal is to make the site easier to write for and more representative of the full creative practice.

Will is currently participating in **STUDIO WORKS**, an experimental studio incubator bringing artists and creative technologists together to develop meaningful, commercially viable objects that can sustain their practices. Over ten weeks, participants move from idea through prototype to production and sale, learning from artist-led studios and building alongside each other.

This week's question: **What should I make?**

Program context supplied by Will: “Developing objects people want from the ideas artists care about.” Presented with support from DFOS and Metalabel. Reference: [TIAT](https://tiat.dfos.com/). The page could not be accessed during the conversation; this summary uses Will's description.

## Direction Will likes

Frame the site as the home of a **creative studio**, with **Work / Studio / Notes** as its main destinations.

The studio should have room for software, objects, sculpture, skateboarding, print, installations, and gatherings. Finding a connection between these activities is still an open question. A single medium or product category does not need to define the practice.

| Destination | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Work | A deliberate selection of projects: finished work, experiments, and eventually things available to buy. |
| Studio | Interests, background, ways of working, and contact information. |
| Notes | Research, observations, work in progress, and occasional STUDIO WORKS updates. |

The homepage can introduce the practice briefly, show four to six representative works, and include a short “In the studio” update. Detailed biography belongs on Studio; current updates should have one home instead of appearing repeatedly throughout the site.

This direction shifts the emphasis from the earlier software-employment portfolio advice in `PORTFOLIO-SUGGESTIONS.md`. Professional experience can still appear, but the creative practice is the organizing frame.

## Writing without turning the archive into a burden

Start by writing short descriptions across a small selection of work, then expand the strongest stories. Do not wait for the entire archive to be complete.

For each selected project:

1. What did I make? Start with a clear sentence and a strong image.
2. What prompted it? Explain the need, curiosity, or circumstances.
3. What did I contribute? Be specific, especially for collaborations.
4. What am I exploring? Describe a meaningful decision, difficulty, or story.
5. What happened? Describe the result, what I learned, or its current state.

A useful first draft can be just: **What I made. What prompted it. What I'm exploring through it.** Two good paragraphs and a few captioned images can be enough. Prototypes and unfinished experiments can communicate a practice too.

Let selected work carry the introduction. Keep the broader archive available without requiring every entry to become a polished case study.

## Simpler links and publishing

Will does not like maintaining Obsidian deep links. The linking system was a fun idea, but it does not help with writing. **Regular hyperlinks are sufficient.**

- Use ordinary site paths when linking to published work, such as `/projects/skateboard.fyi`.
- Link to external references directly.
- Link between projects when the connection helps the reader.
- Mentions of places, people, and interests do not automatically need links or dedicated pages.
- Publish a deliberate selection of work and writing. Research and private fragments can stay wherever writing feels easiest.

Markdown can remain the writing format. Existing link conversion can keep older content working while pages are revised gradually. No migration or route changes have been authorized or implemented by this document.

## Working name: Simons Development Labs

Will proposed **Simons Development Labs**, abbreviated **SDL**. This is a candidate, not a final naming decision.

Editorial assessment: “Labs” gives room for experimentation across media. “Development” can encompass developing ideas into objects, although it may suggest software or a technical consultancy to some readers. A concrete studio description and the selected work can establish the broader meaning.

This research identifies acronym overlap; it does not establish availability of the full name, domains, or trademarks.
